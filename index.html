What is an Ecosystem?

Ecosystem An ecosystem is a place where living things and non-living things live and interact with each other.

“Interact” is a big word. It only means: they act on each other. For example, a rabbit eats grass. The rabbit and the grass interact. Rain falls on the soil. Water and soil interact.

A forest is an ecosystem. A pond is an ecosystem. A garden is an ecosystem. Even a small aquarium in a classroom is a small ecosystem.

Every ecosystem has two kinds of parts. Let us learn both.

🌿 Living things — Biotic components

Biotic means living. Anything that is alive, grows, needs food and can die.

🌱 Plants🐇 Animals🍄 Fungi🦠 Bacteria

💧 Non-living things — Abiotic components

Abiotic means not living. It does not grow, does not eat and cannot die.

☀️ Sunlight💧 Water💨 Air 🟤 Soil🌡️ Temperature

Energy in an Ecosystem

Living things need energy to live.

You need energy to walk, to run, to think and even to sleep. A rabbit needs energy to hop. A tree needs energy to grow leaves. Energy is the power that makes living things work.

We get our energy from food. So the big question is:

Where does most energy in an ecosystem begin?

☀️ It begins with the Sun.

The Sun is the biggest source of energy for almost every ecosystem on Earth.

🎬 Animation: energy moves from the Sun to a plant

What the animation shows: the glowing yellow dots are energy. They move from the Sun to the plant. We write this simply as SUN → PLANT. The Sun gives light energy to plants.

🌿 How does a plant use sunlight? (Photosynthesis)

Photosynthesis Green plants make their own food using sunlight. This process is called photosynthesis.

Say it like this: photo means light, and synthesis means making something. So photosynthesis means “making food with light”.

In simple words: Sunlight + Plant → Food. The plant also uses water from the soil and air. It makes food inside its green leaves. You do not need the chemistry now — just remember that green plants make their own food using sunlight.

Producer

Producer A producer is a living thing that makes its own food.

The word produce means to make. So a producer makes its own food. It does not need to eat another living thing.

What the animation shows: yellow energy dots from the Sun reach the grass and the plant. The green glow means they are making food inside their leaves.

Examples of producers:

🌿 Grass🌳 Trees🟢 Algae🌱 Green plants

🌱 Plants = Producers

Producers usually form the first level of a food chain. They are always at the start, because they are the ones that first catch the Sun’s energy and turn it into food. Without producers, the other living things would have nothing to eat.

Another name: producers are sometimes called autotrophs. “Auto” means self and “troph” means food — self-feeders. You do not have to remember this word, but now you know it.

Consumer

Consumer A consumer is a living thing that cannot make its own food. It gets energy by eating plants or other animals.

The word consume means to eat or use up. Every animal is a consumer. You are a consumer too, because you cannot make food inside your body using sunlight — you must eat.

Both animals are consumers. The rabbit eats a plant. The frog eats another animal. Neither of them can make its own food.

Levels of consumers

We give consumers different names depending on what they eat. Read these three boxes slowly.

1️⃣ Primary Consumer (“primary” = first)

An animal that eats a producer.

Example: Grass → Grasshopper. The grasshopper eats grass (a producer), so the grasshopper is the primary consumer.

2️⃣ Secondary Consumer (“secondary” = second)

An animal that eats a primary consumer.

Example: Grass → Grasshopper → Frog. The frog eats the grasshopper, so the frog is the secondary consumer.

3️⃣ Tertiary Consumer (“tertiary” = third)

An animal that eats a secondary consumer.

Example: Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake. The snake eats the frog, so the snake is the tertiary consumer.

Very important: these levels can change! The name depends on what the animal is eating at that time. A bird that eats seeds is a primary consumer. The same bird eating a caterpillar is a secondary consumer. So always look at the food chain in front of you before you name the level.
Tiny check: In Grass → Rabbit → Fox, what is the rabbit?

Decomposers

Decomposer Decomposers break down dead plants and animals.

“Break down” means they eat and rot it into very small pieces. Think of an old banana peel on the ground. After some days it turns black, then soft, then it mixes into the soil. Decomposers did that work.

Examples of decomposers:

🍄 Fungi (like mushrooms)🦠 Bacteria

They return nutrients to the soil.

Nutrients are the useful materials that living things need to grow — a little like vitamins for plants. Plants take these nutrients from the soil through their roots.

What the animation shows: dead leaves fall to the ground. Fungi and bacteria break them down. Green dots (nutrients) travel into the soil. The roots of a plant take those nutrients and the plant grows.

The recycling path

Decomposers are extremely important. Without them, dead plants and animals would pile up and never disappear, and the nutrients inside them would be locked away forever. Decomposers recycle those nutrients so plants can use them again. Recycle means use again.

What is a Food Chain?

Food chain A food chain shows who eats whom in an ecosystem.
It also shows… how energy moves from one living thing to another.

Think of a real chain made of metal rings. Each ring is joined to the next ring. A food chain is the same idea, but each “ring” is a living thing, and they are joined by eating.

🎬 A grassland food chain, step by step

Press Play to watch energy move through the chain.
The steps in words:
  1. Step 1: Sun gives energy to grass.
  2. Step 2: Grasshopper eats grass.
  3. Step 3: Frog eats grasshopper.
  4. Step 4: Snake eats frog.
  5. Step 5: Eagle eats snake.
Look at the energy bars. Each time you go one step up the chain, the bar becomes shorter. This is because an animal uses most of the energy from its food for moving, breathing and keeping warm. Only a small part of the energy is stored in its body and passed to the next animal. So the higher you go in a food chain, the less energy is available. That is why there are many grasshoppers in a field but only a few eagles.

Very Important: What Do Food Chain Arrows Mean?

Many students get this wrong, so read this part slowly. It is easy once you understand it.

Look at this:

❓ What does the arrow mean?

The arrow shows the direction that ENERGY moves.

Energy moves from the grass → to the grasshopper.

The arrow points toward the organism that is receiving the energy — that is, toward the one doing the eating.

Easy trick: read the arrow as the words “is eaten by”.
Grass → Grasshopper = “Grass is eaten by the grasshopper.”
It does not mean “grass eats the grasshopper”. Grass cannot eat!

One more example:

The snake eats the frog, so energy moves from the frog to the snake. We write it as Frog → Snake.

🎯 Activity: choose the correct arrow

Read the sentence. Then choose the arrow that is written correctly. Take your time.

Trophic Levels (Feeding Levels)

Trophic level A trophic level is a feeding level in a food chain.

The word trophic just means “to do with feeding”. Every living thing in a food chain stands on one “step”, and each step is a trophic level. We usually draw them as a pyramid, because there is a lot of energy at the bottom and only a little at the top.

Read the pyramid from the bottom up. The bottom level is the widest because it has the most available energy. As you go up, each level is thinner because less energy is left. Most of the energy is used by the animals for moving and breathing, and some is lost as heat.
Simple picture: if the grass has 1000 units of energy, the grasshoppers may only get about 100, the frogs about 10, and the snake about 1. The numbers are not exact — they only show that energy gets smaller and smaller as we go up.

What is a Food Web?

Food web A food web is made of many food chains connected together.

Why do we need a food web?

Animals usually eat more than one type of food.

A snake does not only eat frogs. It may also eat mice. An eagle does not only eat snakes. It may also eat rabbits and small birds. So one single food chain is not enough to show the truth. When we draw all the food chains of one place together, we get a food web.

🎬 Watch three food chains join into one food web

Press Play to start.
Chain 1: Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake  ·  Chain 2: Grass → Rabbit → Eagle  ·  Chain 3: Seeds → Mouse → Snake → Eagle
Notice that grass appears in two chains and the snake and eagle appear in two chains. Those shared organisms are what join the chains together.

When many food chains connect, they form a food web.

Tiny check: Why is a food web more true to real life than one food chain?

Food Chain vs Food Web

🔗 Food Chain

  • One feeding path
  • Simple
  • Shows one route of energy

Example: Grass → Rabbit → Fox

🕸️ Food Web

  • Many food chains connected
  • More complex
  • Shows many routes of energy

Example: many chains sharing the same organisms

A food chain is one path.
A food web is many connected paths.

Comparison of a food chain and a food web
PointFood ChainFood Web
How many paths?Only oneMany
How does it look?One straight lineMany crossing lines
How difficult?Simple and easy to drawMore complex
Is it like real life?A small part of real lifeMuch closer to real life
If one animal is removed…The whole chain breaksOther paths may still work

What Happens if One Organism Disappears?

Now let us do an experiment. Below is a small food web:

  • Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Eagle
  • and also Grass → Rabbit → Eagle

Press a button to remove one animal, and watch what happens to the others. Then press Restore Ecosystem to bring everything back.

How many are left? (population)

Population means how many of them there are. Watch the bars go up or down.

Nothing has changed yet. The ecosystem is healthy. 🌿

Example — remove the grasshopper:
“Frogs may have less food.” → frog numbers go down.
“If frogs decrease, snakes may also have less food.” → snake numbers go down.
“Fewer snakes means the eagle must eat more rabbits.” → the change keeps travelling through the web.
Also, with no grasshoppers eating it, the grass may grow more.

All organisms in an ecosystem can be connected.

One small change in one place can change many other living things. This is why we must protect every part of an ecosystem, even the small insects.

Tiny check: If all the frogs disappear, what will most likely happen to the snakes?

What Happens if the Producers Disappear?

This is the most serious case of all. Press the red button and watch the whole ecosystem slowly become empty.

The ecosystem is healthy right now.

Press the red button to start the simulation.

Producers bring energy into most food chains.

Without producers, plant-eating animals would lose their food. When those animals become fewer, the animals that eat them would also be affected. Step by step, the whole ecosystem falls apart. This is why grass, plants, trees and algae are so precious — they are the doorway through which the Sun’s energy enters the living world.

Predator and Prey

PredatorAn animal that hunts and eats another animal.
PreyAn animal that is hunted and eaten.

Example: A snake eats a frog.
So Snake = Predator and Frog = Prey.

🎬 The same animal can be both!

This surprises many students, so watch carefully. The frog will change its role in front of your eyes.

Press Play.
Scene 1: Frog eats grasshopper → the frog is the predator, the grasshopper is the prey.
Scene 2: Snake eats frog → now the frog is the prey, and the snake is the predator.
So the words predator and prey describe what is happening right now, not a fixed name for life.
Tiny check: An eagle catches a snake. Who is the prey?

Herbivore, Carnivore and Omnivore

These three words simply tell us what kind of food an animal eats. Tap a card to see its animals move.

Word help: “herb” means plant, “carn” means meat, and “omni” means everything. So a herbivore eats plants, a carnivore eats meat, and an omnivore eats both.
