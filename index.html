OpenAI’s new model: Astra

Astra is not public yet. We only know about it from OpenAI’s own posts and from news reports.

OpenAI 1–2 August 2026

Astra solved 10 old maths problems

OpenAI wrote a blog post about maths. Inside that post, it named its next big model for the first time: Astra. An internal version of Astra found answers to 10 problems in maths and computer science. People had been stuck on each of them for at least ten years, and on most of them for much longer.

OpenAI published a 249-page paper and put machine-checkable proofs on GitHub, written in a proof language called Lean 4. A computer can check every step, and no step is left open. Three of the problems came from the famous list of the mathematician Paul Erdős.

249Pages of proof
$2,000Compute cost, all 10
0Peer reviews so far

Why this matters

The answers are checked by software, so they are hard to fake. But no human expert has reviewed them yet in a journal, and $2,000 of computer time is very cheap for work of this kind. If it holds up, AI just became a real research tool for maths.

Safety 7 August 2026

OpenAI slowed its own work on Astra

OpenAI tested Astra and could not rule out that it had reached the “Critical” level for cyber skills. That is the top risk level in OpenAI’s own safety rulebook, the Preparedness Framework. In the roughly three years the framework has existed, no model had ever reached this point.

“Critical” means the model could find and build working attacks against strong, well-defended real systems on its own — or plan and run a whole cyberattack when it is only told the goal. So OpenAI stopped the parts of its Astra work that did not meet its stricter new security rules. It also turned on monitoring for risky behaviour everywhere the model acts on its own, including during training and testing.

Why this matters

This is the first time a lab has said in public that its own model may be too capable at hacking to keep working on normally. OpenAI said it shared the result because the public and security teams should know.

OpenAI Still open

Nobody knows what Astra will be called

OpenAI calls Astra its “next major model” and says it is built for long jobs that run for a while. It has not said what name Astra will ship under. It could be GPT-6. It could be a smaller step like GPT-5.7. It could become a fourth tier next to the current Sol, Terra and Luna models. There is no release date.

Not confirmed

Some social media posts claim Astra is GPT-6 and will arrive within days. That is a leak, not an announcement. Treat it as a rumour until OpenAI says so.

ChatGPT 4–7 August 2026

GPT-5.6 arrived, and free users got more

OpenAI released GPT-5.6 on 4 August. It gives more quality for the money than the model before it. On 7 August, OpenAI improved GPT-5.6 Sol inside ChatGPT and opened up wider access to GPT-5.6 Luna for people on the free plan.

OpenAI also started a beta of “Sign in with ChatGPT”, so you can log in to other sites with your ChatGPT account. Airtable, GitLab, HubSpot, Notion, Supabase and Vercel are in the first group. Anthropic and Claude

Anthropic makes Claude. Its recent news is less about raw power and more about work, control and trust.

Anthropic 24 July 2026

Claude Opus 5 is out on every Anthropic platform

Claude Opus 5 is now the normal model for Claude Max users, the strongest choice on Claude Pro, and available to developers through the API. Anthropic says it comes close to Fable, its most powerful model, on many tasks — but costs half as much.

It also has an effort switch. You choose low, medium or high, and the model spends more or less thinking on your task. Low effort is cheap and quick; high effort costs more and works harder.

Why this matters

Anthropic calls Opus 5 its most aligned Opus model and the hardest one to trick into misuse. In an automated behaviour audit it scored 2.3 — the lowest rate of bad behaviour among current models. Lower is better here.

Anthropic August 2026

New tools for companies and governments

Claude Cowork now works on mobile and on the web, so your sessions and files follow you between devices. It can run tasks in the background, run them on a schedule, share chats and projects, and let you approve steps from your phone.

For large companies, Anthropic added inference hooks in beta. These check prompts and tool calls before they reach the model, so a compliance team can block sensitive data in real time across chat, Claude Code and Cowork. Teams can also run Claude Code on their own servers now, in a public beta, with access to their internal network. And Claude for Government is in beta, with Anthropic billing agencies directly, so they do not need a separate cloud contract.

Words to know

Short meanings for the terms used above.

Model
The AI system itself. GPT-5.6, Astra and Claude Opus 5 are all models.
Frontier model
One of the most capable models that exists right now.
Token
A small piece of text, roughly part of a word. Companies charge by the million tokens.
Agentic
The model does not just answer. It takes steps and uses tools on its own.
Zero-day
A security hole nobody has fixed yet, because nobody knew about it.
Lean proof
A maths proof written so a computer can check every single step.
Alignment
How well a model follows the rules and intentions it was given.
Peer review
Other experts read the work and check it before a journal publishes it.
